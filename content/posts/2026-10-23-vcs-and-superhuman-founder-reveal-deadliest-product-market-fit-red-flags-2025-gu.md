## The PMF Illusion: Why Startups Are Deceiving Themselves in 2025

Finding product-market fit (PMF) has always been the holy grail of tech entrepreneurship, but in 2025, the goalposts have shifted dramatically. With AI enabling rapid code generation and low-code platforms flooding app stores, launching a polished product takes days rather than quarters. Yet, despite frictionless launches, tech startups are failing at historic rates.

Why? Founders confuse vanity traction with genuine product-market fit. 

Rahul Vohra, founder and CEO of Superhuman, alongside prominent Silicon Valley venture capitalists from firms like Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia, and Benchmark, have recently sounded the alarm on subtle yet fatal indicators that founders overlook. If you are building software, evaluating tech investments, or simply trying to understand why your favorite tools get shuttered out of nowhere, these expert-backed red flags reveal the unvarnished reality of survival in the modern software landscape.

---

## Rahul Vohra’s Litmus Test: The 40% Rule

Before diving into investor warnings, it pays to revisit Rahul Vohra’s legendary Product-Market Fit Engine. While building Superhuman—the lightning-fast email client aimed at high-velocity knowledge workers—Vohra sought a mathematical, repeatable benchmark for PMF rather than relying on gut feeling.

Vohra adapted Sean Ellis’s classic question: *"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"*
- A) Very disappointed
- B) Somewhat disappointed
- C) Not disappointed

According to Vohra, the premier red flag is scoring below 40% on the "Very Disappointed" segment. If your user base does not hit that threshold, scaling marketing spend or hiring outbound sales reps will act like throwing gasoline on wet wood. In recent 2025 keynotes, Vohra highlighted that many founders falsely average out feedback across all users instead of segmenting their happiest cohort. If your core evangelists don't fiercely defend your product's existence, you do not have PMF—full stop.

---

## 4 Fatal PMF Red Flags Identified by Top VCs

Beyond Vohra's metric, venture partners are spotting distinct patterns in pitch decks and telemetry dashboards that spell doom before a Series A or B round.

### 1. The "Leaky Bucket" Disguised by Paid Acquisition
Top VCs frequently note that early-stage founders hide poor retention behind aggressive customer acquisition cost (CAC) spend. If month-three retention falls off a cliff, adding more top-of-funnel users only burns cash faster. A steady retention curve that flattens horizontally is proof of PMF; a curve that steadily slopes toward zero is a death march.

### 2. Feature Creep for Single Enterprise Whales
When early-stage SaaS teams start custom-building bespoke integrations just to close a single mid-market deal, investors get nervous. While enterprise revenue looks great on a quarterly spreadsheet, building custom software turns you into an agency rather than a scalable product company.

### 3. Usage Driven by Incentives, Not Core Value
In the Web3 and consumer-app worlds, token drops, heavy referral bonuses, and artificial gamification often disguise a hollow product. If usage tanks the moment promotional incentives taper off, users were never there for your utility—they were there for the bounty.

### 4. Zero Word-of-Mouth Organic Pull
If your Net Promoter Score is hovering in the single digits and you track zero organic search or peer referrals, you haven't built something remarkable. VCs want to see founders who have customers dragging their colleagues into the workspace organically.

## How to Pivot When Red Flags Surface

Spotting these warning signs early is a superpower, not a death sentence. When Superhuman faced mediocre numbers early on, Vohra didn't throw in the towel—he deliberately restricted growth, segmented his target persona to founders who actually felt email overload, and redesigned the onboarding experience to focus exclusively on keyboard shortcuts.

If your telemetry warns of low retention:
1. **Stop Acquisition:** Halt paid ad channels immediately.
2. **Interview Churned Users:** Find out precisely where expectations decoupled from reality.
3. **Double Down on the "Very Disappointed":** Ignore casual users who demand peripheral features and obsess over the minority who genuinely rely on your core workflow.
