## Introduction

There was a golden era of real-time strategy (RTS) games in the late 1990s and early 2000s. Before the genre was somewhat sidelined by MOBAs and battle royales, developers were taking massive creative risks. One of the absolute pinnacles of this era was released in the summer of 2004: *Ground Control II: Operation Exodus*. Developed by Massive Entertainment (the brilliant minds who would later give us *World in Conflict* and Ubisoft's *The Division*), this game stripped away the tediousness of base-building to focus entirely on tactical, squad-based battlefield warfare.

If you missed out on this masterpiece twenty years ago, or if you are looking to relive the glory days of tactical sci-fi combat, GOG (Good Old Games) has an incredible surprise for you. ## Why Ground Control II: Operation Exodus Still Holds Up

Unlike traditional RTS games of its era, such as *Command & Conquer* or *StarCraft*, *Ground Control II* does not ask you to mine minerals, harvest gas, or build barracks. Instead, you are given a limited pool of acquisition points, which you earn by capturing Victory Locations and destroying enemy units. You spend these points to call in dropships loaded with customizable reinforcements.

This design choice shifts the entire focus of the game to pure battlefield tactics. You must utilize cover, manage line-of-sight, deploy secondary weapon modes, and leverage high-ground advantages. The game features two distinct playable factions: the human Northern Alliance and the alien Viron invaders, each with vastly different units and playstyles. 

Visually, *Ground Control II* was a benchmark title in 2004. Thanks to Massive Entertainment's proprietary proprietary engine, the game featured fully 3D battlefields, gorgeous weather effects, and highly detailed unit models that still look surprisingly clean today. When you zoom the camera down to the dirt level to watch your infantry exchange plasma fire with alien tanks, it is easy to forget this game is two decades old.
