## Introduction: The LGA 1700 Socket Refuses to Die

Just when we thought Intel was ready to fully migrate to the new LGA 1851 socket with the launch of Arrow Lake (Core Ultra 200), the rumor mill has dropped a bombshell for budget-conscious PC builders and upgraders. Intel is reportedly planning to breathe new life into the aging LGA 1700 platform in 2025 with a refresh lineup internally dubbed 'Raptor Lake Next' (often associated with the Bartlett Lake-S silicon). 

Rather than adopting the premium 'Core Ultra' branding, these processors will reportedly retain the standard 'Core 200' moniker. According to recent hardware leaks, this desktop lineup will top out at 20 cores, but the real star of the show is a rumored, highly specialized 10-core SKU boasting 24MB of L3 cache. For gamers and mainstream users, this could be the ultimate budget upgrade path of 2025.

---

## Decoding 'Raptor Lake Next' and the Core 200 Branding

Intel's naming scheme has undergone a massive shift recently, but the company is keeping things surprisingly straightforward for this LGA 1700 refresh. The 'Raptor Lake Next' chips will slot underneath the Core Ultra 200 series, serving as an affordable, mid-range alternative. 

By releasing these chips under the 'Core 200' non-Ultra family, Intel is targeting users who want modern performance without the premium price tag associated with DDR5-only Z890 motherboards and Arrow Lake CPUs. Because Raptor Lake Next utilizes the LGA 1700 socket, these processors will be fully compatible with existing H610, B660, H770, and Z790 motherboards (likely requiring only a simple BIOS update). This allows builders to reuse their cheaper DDR4 or DDR5 memory, saving hundreds of dollars on a platform upgrade.

---

## The Spec Sheet: A 20-Core Ceiling and the 10-Core Gaming Unicorn

According to prominent hardware leakers, the top-tier SKU in the Raptor Lake Next lineup will feature a maximum configuration of 20 cores. This is expected to be a hybrid design consisting of 8 Performance cores (P-cores) and 12 Efficient cores (E-cores). While this is slightly fewer cores than the 24-core i9-14900K, it allows Intel to optimize yields and offer highly competitive pricing for mid-range buyers.

However, the SKU that has hardware enthusiasts incredibly excited is a rumored 10-core, 10-thread processor. Unlike Intel's traditional hybrid architectures, this unique chip is rumored to feature **10 pure P-cores and 0 E-cores**, coupled with a generous 24MB of L3 cache. 

### Why a 10-Core P-Core Chip is a Big Deal for Gamers

For years, PC gamers have debated the utility of Intel's Efficient cores (E-cores). While E-cores are fantastic for background tasks and multi-threaded rendering workloads, they can sometimes introduce scheduling headaches in games, leading to micro-stuttering or lower 1% low frame rates. 

A pure 10-core/20-thread (or 10-core/10-thread, depending on hyper-threading configurations) CPU eliminates these scheduling issues entirely. With 24MB of L3 cache shared directly among 10 high-performance Raptor Cove cores, this processor could offer blistering gaming performance that rivals or beats older flagships like the Core i9-12900K, all while running cooler and consuming less power.

## Bottom Line / Our Verdict

Intel's decision to launch 'Raptor Lake Next' under the Core 200 banner is a brilliant chess move. It acknowledges that not everyone is ready to abandon their DDR4 memory and LGA 1700 motherboards for the expensive entry price of the LGA 1851 socket. 

While the 20-core flagship will offer a solid upgrade path for productivity users, the rumored **10-core, pure P-core SKU with 24MB of L3 cache** is shaping up to be the sleeper hit of 2025. If Intel prices this chip aggressively—ideally around the $200 to $240 mark—it could easily become the go-to recommendation for budget gaming builds, giving AMD's Ryzen 5 7600X and 9600X a serious run for their money. If you are currently on a 12th-gen Intel CPU and want one final, meaningful upgrade without rebuilding your entire PC, we highly recommend holding onto your LGA 1700 motherboard and waiting to see what Intel delivers in early 2025.
