## Unboxing History: The 97-Cartridge Time Capsule

### The Rare Variants

What makes this discovery truly monumental is the presence of rare early-run manufacturing variants. During the early launch window of the NES in North America (1985–1987), Nintendo experimented with several packaging iterations:

1. **Hangtab and Gloss Seals**: Early Black Box games featured cardboard hangtabs built directly into the box and clear circular sticker seals.
2. **5-Screw Cartridges**: Before Nintendo switched to 3-screw casings with plastic clips, original NES cartridges used five slotted brass screws. 
3. **Code Revisions**: Several *Duck Hunt* and *Super Mario Bros.* cartridges in this batch carry code prints on the labels that predate widely documented retail releases.

Five of these cartridges achieved the holy grail of collectible grading: a **PSA 10 Gem Mint** rating. In the world of video game collecting, a PSA 10 copy of an early Black Box *Super Mario Bros.* or standalone *Duck Hunt* can easily fetch upwards of $50,000 to $100,000 at auction.
