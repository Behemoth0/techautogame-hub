## From Cosmic Biomes to Workplace Laughs: The Joe Bennett Touch

If you spent any part of late 2023 and 2024 falling down the intoxicating, terrifying rabbit hole of *Scavengers Reign*, you already know the name Joe Bennett. The visionary animator helped deliver one of the most stunning, organically intricate sci-fi epics of our generation. When Netflix rescued the show after its unceremonious cancellation at Max, audiences flooded the platform, hoping for a renewal.

While a direct second season of *Scavengers Reign* remains in cosmic limbo, Netflix announced an entirely unexpected next move for Bennett: a half-hour adult animated workplace comedy titled **'Dealies'** (co-created with Steve Hely, known for *Veep* and *American Dad!*). Slated to capture audiences throughout 2025, *Dealies* moves Bennett from hyper-detailed alien ecosystems into the mundane, deeply absurd environment of everyday corporate grind.

For gamers and sci-fi devotees who fell in love with Bennett’s tactile, rotoscope-adjacent art style, the shift to a punchline-driven, situational comedy might raise an eyebrow. But taking a closer look at Bennett’s viral shorts and early indie work reveals that dry, surreal, and deadpan comedic timing has always run through his creative veins.

## Why the Gaming Community Loves Bennett’s Visual Alchemy

The overlap between Joe Bennett enthusiasts and indie gaming communities is massive. Video games have long pushed the envelope of expressive biological art—think of the fungal surrealism in *Morrowind*, the alien biomes of *Subnautica*, or the evocative line art in *Sable* and *Moebius*-inspired titles. Bennett’s visuals communicate weight, texture, and environmental storytelling in the exact way visionary game designers do.

In *Dealies*, early reports suggest Bennett won’t abandon his trademark observational nuance. While *Scavengers Reign* focused on human beings adapting to lethal biological symbiosis, *Dealies* turns that lens onto modern corporate bureaucracy. The endless meetings, forced team synergies, and soul-crushing corporate jargon become their own kind of hostile alien ecosystem. For those of us who juggle marathon gaming sessions with white-collar remote jobs, the premise hits almost uncomfortably close to home.

## The Convergence of Indie Games and High-Concept Animation

We are living in an unprecedented golden age where the aesthetics of indie gaming and prestige adult animation are virtually indistinguishable. Studio Trigger's work on *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners*, Fortiche's boundary-pushing production on *Arcane*, and now Joe Bennett's transition across mainstream streaming demonstrate how vital visual identity has become.

When Bennett directs an animated scene, characters interact with their environment using grounded, realistic physics. If *Dealies* applies that same meticulous kinetic detail to petty office squabbles—the hum of a faulty fluorescent light, the awkward physics of a jammed office printer, or the twitch of an exhausted manager's eye—we are in for a comedic masterclass. Gamers who appreciate the environmental comedy of titles like *Stanley Parable* or *Going Under* will feel right at home with Bennett's unique approach to corporate absurdity.

## What We Hope to See from ‘Dealies’

Transitioning from cosmic dread to workplace satire is a bold artistic leap, but Joe Bennett has never followed predictable paths. As *Dealies* heads toward its debut, the biggest hope from both the animation and gaming crowds is that Netflix lets Bennett's raw, idiosyncratic vision stay intact without sanding down the weird edges.

Corporate comedy has felt stale ever since *The Office* wrapped, with dozens of imitators attempting the same mockumentary tropes. If Bennett injects *Dealies* with his uncanny, slightly unsettling pacing, rich character designs, and surreal observational wit, it could redefine what streaming adult animation looks like for the remainder of the decade.

## Bottom Line / Our Verdict

*Dealies* marks a fascinating turning point in Joe Bennett’s career. While fans might secretly still yearn for more cosmic survival horror, letting an auteur animator of Bennett's caliber dismantle modern workplace culture with sharp, dry comedy is an experiment we eagerly welcome. Whether you're a hardcore gamer who appreciates world-class visual craft or an animation purist looking for fresh laughs, *Dealies* is shaping up to be one of Netflix's must-watch animated releases in 2025.
