## Introduction: The PC Enthusiast's Secret Weapon

If you have been watching the custom PC space over the past couple of years, you have probably noticed a massive shift in how enthusiasts build and customize their rigs. Off-the-shelf brackets and generic aesthetic accessories are out; bespoke, functional, 3D-printed components are in. Whether it is drafting a targeted fan shroud to drop VRM temps by 6 degrees, fabricating a custom anti-sag bracket for a hefty RTX 4090, or printing a complete Small Form Factor (SFF) chassis, 3D printing has become an indispensable extension of the PC building hobby.

At the center of this revolution sits Bambu Lab. For years, 3D printing felt like a secondary hobby requiring endless tinkering, bed-leveling dials, and troubleshooting failed layers. Bambu Lab flipped that script by delivering plug-and-play speed and reliability. Right now, one of the company's most coveted setups—the **Bambu Lab P1S Combo with the AMS (Automatic Material System)**—is currently available for **$70 off**, dropping the price from its typical $949 MSRP down to a much more enticing **$879**.

If you have been waiting on the sidelines for the right moment to jump into high-speed, multi-material 3D printing in 2025, this deal deserves your immediate attention.

## Why the Bambu Lab P1S Remains a Benchmark

The Bambu Lab P1S sits in the absolute sweet spot of the company's lineup. Positioned between the entry-level open-frame A1 series and the flagship X1-Carbon, the P1S delivers roughly 95% of the X1-Carbon's real-world capability at a substantially lower price.

### Fully Enclosed CoreXY Performance
Unlike traditional "bed-slinger" printers where the build plate moves forward and backward along the Y-axis, the P1S utilizes an ultra-rigid CoreXY motion system. The print head darts effortlessly along carbon fiber rods while the print bed only moves incrementally downward along the Z-axis. With acceleration rates up to 20,000 mm/s² and top print speeds reaching 500 mm/s, prints that used to take 14 hours on legacy machines finish cleanly in under four.

Crucially, the P1S features an enclosed chassis with an active cooling fan, carbon air filter, and dedicated chamber regulator. For PC builders, this enclosure is vital. Standard materials like PLA and PETG are fine for basic desk toys, but high-heat environments—like resting right beneath a 300W graphics card or clamping onto a hot water-cooling radiator—demand technical filaments like ABS, ASA, or carbon-fiber-reinforced blends (PET-CF). The enclosed environment prevents warping and layer separation, guaranteeing structural integrity for your custom hardware.

## The Real Star: The AMS Multi-Material System

While you can buy the standalone P1S for around $599, purchasing the **P1S Combo with the AMS** is the definitive way to experience this machine. The AMS sits on top of the printer and houses four filament spools simultaneously.

For PC builders, the AMS is not just about printing colorful figurines (though it excels at that, too). Its real superpower lies in functional manufacturing:

- **Dedicated Support Interfaces:** You can print complex overhangs—such as intricate GPU fan ducts or custom cable routing channels—using PLA for the main body and PETG (or specialized support material) exclusively for the interface layer. Because PETG and PLA do not bond chemically, support structures snap off effortlessly with zero scarring, leaving injection-mold-quality surfaces.
- **Seamless Filament Runout Switching:** The AMS automatically switches to a backup spool of the same filament when one runs dry mid-print. No more half-finished PC brackets wasted overnight.
- **Multi-Tone Branding:** Want your custom reservoir bracket to sport a dual-color aesthetic that perfectly matches your motherboard’s ROG, AORUS, or ProArt color scheme? The AMS handles up to 16 colors (with multiple units linked) with zero manual intervention.

## Essential PC Modding Projects to Print on Day One

Once you unbox and run the 15-minute automated self-calibration on the P1S, here are three essential PC projects you can tackle immediately:

1. **Direct-Die and M.2 Fan Mounts:** High-speed PCIe 5.0 NVMe SSDs run notoriously hot. Using heat-resistant ABS filament, you can print a clip-on bracket that mounts a tiny 40mm Noctua fan directly over your motherboard's primary M.2 heatsink.
2. **Custom SFF Travel Braces:** If you build Mini-ITX systems for LAN parties, standard components shift during transit. A custom-modeled TPU (flexible rubber) standoff prevents your GPU from flexing in its PCIe slot while on the road.
3. **Cable Routing Ducts & Combs:** Clean up your PSU cabling with bespoke chassis-mounted combs printed in matte black or neon accents, routing 12V-2x6 and 24-pin ATX cables cleanly behind the motherboard tray.
