## Introduction: The New Reality of Tech Restructuring

The narrative surrounding tech industry layoffs has officially shifted. While the massive workforce reductions of 2022 and 2023 were primarily attributed to post-pandemic over-hiring and rising interest rates, the restructuring wave of 2025 has a very different scapegoat: Artificial Intelligence.

Work management software leader Monday.com recently made headlines after announcing strategic realignments and targeted layoffs, pointing directly toward the adoption of internal AI efficiency and automated workflows. The company stated that automated agents, internal generative AI tools, and algorithmic task management have streamlined engineering, customer support, and administrative functions—reducing the need for human headcount in several departments.

However, Monday.com is far from an isolated case. Across the global technology sector, executives are increasingly framing headcount cuts not as financial desperation, but as an aggressive, proactive push toward operational efficiency powered by AI. Here is a deep dive into the 20 other major tech companies that have publicly cited AI automation, efficiency gains, or strategic pivots toward artificial intelligence as key drivers behind recent job eliminations.

## 20 Tech Companies Citing AI for Job Cuts

### 1. Klarna
The buy-now-pay-later giant stunned the tech market by announcing an aggressive hiring freeze alongside headcount reductions, revealing that its internal OpenAI-powered assistant performs the work of roughly 700 full-time customer service agents.

### 2. Duolingo
The popular language-learning platform phased out a significant portion of its contractor workforce, choosing instead to rely on generative AI to create lesson content, write translated text, and generate audio scripts at scale.

### 3. IBM
CEO Arvind Krishna openly shared plans to pause hiring for roles that AI could easily replace, predicting that up to 7,800 back-office HR and administrative jobs could eventually be automated out of existence.

### 4. SAP
As part of a massive transformation program affecting nearly 8,000 employees, the enterprise software company explicitly redirected funds away from traditional roles toward AI development and internal machine-learning integration.

### 5. Chegg
The online education technology provider restructured its operations and laid off a quarter of its workforce as direct-to-consumer generative AI tools like ChatGPT severely altered demand for traditional homework help platforms.

### 6. Google
Despite generating massive revenues, Alphabet restructured teams in engineering, core tech, and hardware, with CEO Sundar Pichai noting that internal coding tools and AI-driven processes are vastly improving internal developer speed.

### 7. Dropbox
Dropbox eliminated 16% of its workforce specifically to pivot resources toward AI-first product lines, stating that traditional cloud storage demands a leaner team while building AI search assistants requires specialized talent.

### 8. Block (Square)
Led by Jack Dorsey, Block implemented strict caps on total headcount, explicitly aiming to leverage internal AI tools and automated design pipelines to maintain productivity with a smaller overall staff.

### 9. Salesforce
Despite strong quarterly earnings, Salesforce reshuffled thousands of roles, shifting resources toward its AI agent platforms while reducing teams dedicated to legacy software maintenance.

### 10. Cisco
Networking infrastructure titan Cisco cut thousands of roles to fund a multi-billion dollar pivot toward AI infrastructure hardware, network security algorithms, and automated telemetry.

### 11. Amazon
Amazon reorganized several divisions, including Alexa development and corporate communications, automating internal reporting processes and leaning heavily into AI-powered logistics routing.

### 12. Meta
Mark Zuckerberg's "Year of Efficiency" extended deep into 2025, using internal AI code generation tools to streamline developer workflows and lower the ratio of managers to individual contributors.

### 13. Microsoft
While investing tens of billions into OpenAI, Microsoft reorganized internal teams within gaming, sales, and modern work divisions, citing streamlined operations driven by Copilot integrations.

### 14. Intuit
The tax and financial software maker laid off over 1,800 workers while simultaneously hiring for AI-focused roles, declaring that automation was necessary to power its AI financial advisors.

### 15. PayPal
PayPal cut roughly 9% of its global workforce to streamline internal operations and implement automated fraud detection models that reduce the need for manual risk assessment.

### 16. Xerox
Legacy printing and workflow leader Xerox announced a 15% workforce reduction as it implemented a new operating model focused heavily on AI-driven IT service automation.

### 17. SoundHound AI
Even native AI companies aren't immune. SoundHound reorganized operations to eliminate legacy voice-processing engineering roles in favor of unified generative voice architectures.

### 18. Shopify
Shopify pushed teams to adopt automated workflow tools, establishing strict benchmarks where managers must prove why AI cannot perform tasks before adding new human headcount.

### 19. Unity Technologies
The game engine developer trimmed nearly 25% of its staff to reset its corporate footprint, leaning on automated asset generation tools and algorithmically driven ad-tech pipelines.

### 20. Toast
The restaurant software ecosystem cut hundreds of positions, streamlining customer onboarding and technical support using conversational AI routing engines.
