## Introduction

The electric vehicle landscape is about to get a lot more competitive, and a lot more stylish. Cupra, the high-performance spin-off of Spanish manufacturer SEAT, has officially confirmed that its highly anticipated all-electric mid-size SUV, the Cupra Tindaya, will enter production in late 2025. 

Positioned as a direct rival to the premium BMW iX3 and the upcoming Porsche Macan EV, the Tindaya represents a massive leap forward for the brand. Known for its rebellious attitude, sharp angular designs, and copper-accented performance vehicles, Cupra is no longer just playing in the mainstream sandbox. With the Tindaya, they are targeting the premium executive tier. Here is everything we know about this exciting new electric SUV, how it stacks up against the competition, and the gear you will need to get the most out of it.

## What is the Cupra Tindaya?

Named after the sacred Tindaya mountain in Fuerteventura, Canary Islands, the Cupra Tindaya is built on the Volkswagen Group’s advanced SSP (Scalable Systems Platform) architecture. This platform allows for faster charging times, superior battery density, and a lower center of gravity compared to the older MEB platform used in the Cupra Born.

Design-wise, the Tindaya is a showstopper. It features Cupra’s signature shark-nose front end, aggressive triangular matrix LED headlights, and a coupe-like roofline that manages to preserve rear headroom. The interior is a masterclass in sporty minimalism, utilizing sustainable materials like Dinamica microfiber and recycled ocean plastics, punctuated by high-end copper stitching and a driver-focused digital cockpit.

Under the hood—or rather, under the floorboards—the Tindaya is expected to offer both single-motor rear-wheel-drive and dual-motor all-wheel-drive (VZ) configurations. The top-tier VZ trim is rumored to push out close to 400 horsepower, delivering a 0-60 mph sprint in just under 4.5 seconds. With an estimated 82 kWh battery pack, drivers can expect a real-world range of approximately 310 miles (500 km) on a single charge.

## Taking on the Heavyweights: Cupra Tindaya vs. BMW iX3

To succeed, the Cupra Tindaya has to beat the benchmark: the BMW iX3. While the German competitor is highly regarded for its refined ride and traditional luxury cabin, it has often been criticized for its somewhat conservative, ICE-derived design. 

Cupra is capitalizing on this exact weakness. The Tindaya offers a far more emotional, avant-garde design language that appeals to younger, tech-focused buyers who want their EV to stand out in a sea of sterile commuter pods. Furthermore, the SSP platform enables 800-volt charging architecture, allowing the Tindaya to juice up from 10% to 80% in just 18 minutes. The current BMW iX3, limited by its 400-volt system, takes closer to 32 minutes for the same charge.

In terms of driving dynamics, Cupra’s engineers have promised a bespoke suspension tune with Dynamic Chassis Control (DCC) to ensure the Tindaya handles like a hot hatch rather than a heavy, bloated SUV.

## Pricing, Availability, and the Road Ahead

While Cupra has not officially locked in the final pricing structure, industry insiders suggest the base model Cupra Tindaya will start at around $62,000 (£52,000), scaling up to $78,000 for the high-performance VZ trim. This positions it aggressively just below the current pricing of the BMW iX3, offering buyers more technology, faster charging, and bolder styling for slightly less money.

Production is slated to begin at the Martorell plant in Spain, with deliveries expected to commence in the fourth quarter of 2025. Cupra is also actively exploring expansion into the North American market, meaning the Tindaya could very well be the spearhead for the brand's US debut.

## Bottom Line / Our Verdict

The Cupra Tindaya is exactly the kind of car the EV market needs right now. It injects a heavy dose of emotion, aggressive styling, and genuine driving dynamics into a segment that has lately felt a bit formulaic. By targeting the BMW iX3, Cupra is punching above its weight, but with the backing of the Volkswagen Group's advanced SSP platform and Cupra's knack for sharp suspension tuning, they have a very real shot at taking the crown. If you are looking for a premium, high-performance electric SUV in 2025 and want to stand out from the crowd, the Tindaya should be at the very top of your watchlist.
