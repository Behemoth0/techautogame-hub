## The Anatomy of Safety: 1987 Track Tech vs. 2025 Road Reality

If you study the interior photos of the 1987 Acura Integra 40 Racer, the safety gear is strictly old-school motorsport: a welded steel roll cage, a five-point racing harness, a fire suppression system, and a rudimentary aluminum bucket seat. 

While these features are highly effective at preventing fatal trauma during a high-speed track rollover, they are actually counterproductive—and often dangerous—for daily street driving. For example, a steel roll cage is designed to be used with a helmet; in a street crash without one, your bare skull hitting a steel bar can be fatal. Likewise, a five-point harness holds your torso rigidly upright, but without a HANS (Head and Neck Support) device, your neck absorbs the full force of a frontal impact.

To make a classic analog car safe for 2025 commuter traffic, we need to look at **preventative safety**—technologies that help you avoid the crash altogether.

## Balancing Heritage with Modern Protection

When retrofitting safety gear into a classic car like the Acura Integra, the goal is **non-destructive installation**. 

* **Wiring:** Avoid cutting into your original 1980s wiring harness. Use fuse taps and dedicated relay circuits to power your dash cams and radar systems.
* **Reversibility:** Opt for adhesive mounts rather than drilling holes into rare OEM plastics. For instance, the Wolfbox mirror straps onto your existing mirror, meaning you can return the car to 100% factory stock in under five minutes.
* **Tires and Brakes:** No amount of digital ADAS will save you if your mechanical connection to the road is compromised. Always pair your new safety tech with modern high-performance tires and upgraded brake pads.
