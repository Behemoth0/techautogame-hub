## Introduction: Samsung's Major Security Crackdown

In early 2025, Samsung took a decisive step toward protecting smart home security by banning apps from its Tizen OS app store that allow users to share or sell their unused internet bandwidth to third parties. Commonly referred to as 'peer-to-peer bandwidth sharing' or 'bandwidth monetization' services, these applications promised users passive income in exchange for turning their connected TVs into residential proxy nodes.

While earning a few extra dollars a month sounded appealing to many consumers, the underlying risks to home network security, data privacy, and bandwidth utilization were massive. Samsung’s official policy update prohibits any application whose primary function or secondary capability involves relaying external internet traffic through the user's home network connection. Here is an in-depth breakdown of why Samsung made this decision, how these apps operate, and what equipment you should consider to keep your home network secure and performing at its best.

## The Hidden Danger of Bandwidth-Sharing Apps

For years, applications like Honeygain, Pawns.app, and EarnApp allowed users on PCs and smartphones to rent out their residential IP addresses. Eventually, developers began publishing versions tailored for Smart TVs and streaming boxes, capitalizing on the fact that smart TVs remain plugged in and connected to Wi-Fi 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

When you install a bandwidth-sharing app on your Samsung Smart TV, your household router becomes a residential proxy. Outside companies, researchers, and anonymous clients route their internet traffic through your IP address. While companies claim this traffic is used for legitimate tasks like web scraping, ad verification, and price comparison, there is rarely any transparent mechanism enforcing those limits.

## How These Apps Threaten Your Network Security

Routing third-party traffic through your home connection carries severe risks that far outweigh a few dollars in monthly rewards:

1. Malicious Traffic and IP Blacklisting: Cybercriminals frequently lease residential proxies to obscure their location. If someone uses your IP address to conduct malicious activities, carry out credential stuffing, or scrape sensitive sites, your IP address—not the criminal's—gets blacklisted by websites, streaming services, and your Internet Service Provider (ISP).
2. ISP Policy Violations: Most consumer internet service providers explicitly ban reselling or sharing residential bandwidth in their Terms of Service. Users running these apps risk bandwidth throttling or complete service termination.
3. Network Degradation: Smart TVs running background proxy services continuously consume upload and download capacity. This leads to latency spikes during gaming, buffering on video streams, and degraded performance across all connected devices in your home.
4. Vulnerability to Exploits: Smart TV operating systems are notoriously slower to receive security patches than smartphones. Running proxy software on a TV creates an enticing vector for local network intrusion.

## What This Means for Tizen OS and Smart TV Owners

Samsung's policy change means any app identified as a bandwidth proxy will be removed from the Tizen Store, and existing installations will be blocked from accessing network sockets. Samsung is prioritizing platform integrity over niche app monetization, signaling to developers that smart TVs should remain dedicated entertainment hubs, not distributed proxy networks.

If you currently own a Samsung Smart TV, you do not need to take manual action regarding official app store downloads, but auditing your installed applications is always good practice. Go to your TV's settings menu, inspect active background apps, and uninstall any third-party tools promising cash rewards for background operation.

## How to Keep Your Smart TV and Home Network Secure

To ensure your home remains safe from unauthorized bandwidth usage, adopt these essential habits:

- Isolate IoT and Smart TVs: Use your router's VLAN or Guest Network feature to separate smart appliances from your personal laptops and storage servers.
- Audit App Permissions: Periodically check installed apps on your TV and remove anything you no longer use.
- Avoid Unofficial Sideloading: Never install APKs or third-party app packages from unverified internet sources on Android TV or Fire OS devices.
- Keep Firmware Updated: Set your Smart TV to automatically download and install system updates so security patches are applied immediately.

## Our Verdict: A Necessary Win for Consumer Security

Samsung’s decision to ban bandwidth-sharing apps on Tizen OS in 2025 is a long-overdue win for consumer security and digital safety. While passive income apps sound alluring on paper, turning a home television into an unmonitored exit node for strangers exposes users to severe security vulnerabilities, potential ISP penalties, and degraded streaming quality. 

By taking a firm stance against background proxy services, Samsung sets a positive example that other smart TV manufacturers like LG, Sony, and TCL should follow. If you value network performance and peace of mind, stick to verified streaming platforms and pair your smart display with a secure home router.

3. **Погіршення роботи мережі:** Телевізори Smart TV із фоновими проксі-сервісами постійно споживають ресурси завантаження та віддачі даних.
