When Nvidia officially launched the RTX 5090 this year, gamer memes instantly flooded the internet. It wasn't just about the eye-watering $2,400 price tag; it was about the physical requirements. The running joke of 2025 is that buying an RTX 5090 isn't a hardware upgrade—it's a home renovation project. You don't plug it into your motherboard; you bolt your motherboard onto the GPU.

Here are the 4 Stages of Nvidia GPU Buying in 2025:

2. Bargaining: "If I sell a kidney and skip eating lunch until November, I can get 12 more frames per second in Cyberpunk."
3. Acceptance: "My local utility company just sent me a handwritten thank-you card for keeping their grid profitable."
4. Regret: "I just spent $2,500 to play Old School RuneScape and watch YouTube on a second monitor."

## "This Toaster Features 45 TOPS of AI Processing"

If 2024 was the year AI walked, 2025 is the year AI forcefully broke down your front door, sat on your couch, and refused to leave. Every single manufacturer at CES this year decided that standard consumer products were useless unless they had a neural processing unit jammed into them.

Gamers are out here spending thousands on top-tier rigs just to stare at a library of 600 unplayed games for 45 minutes before turning off the PC and watching Twitch on a phone.

Tech in 2025 is hilarious, chaotic, and ridiculously expensive. But amidst all the $2,000 graphics cards, useless AI fridges, and endless Steam sales, the joke is always on us—because we know we're still going to buy it all anyway. 

Real advice? Don't fall for the 2025 hype machine. You do not need an AI-powered thermal paste application tool, and your RTX 3070 is still doing just fine. Save your money, actually play three games from your Steam backlog this week, and laugh at the absurd state of modern tech.
