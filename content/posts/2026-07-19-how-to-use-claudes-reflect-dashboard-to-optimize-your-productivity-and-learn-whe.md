## What is Claude's Reflect Dashboard?

The Reflect dashboard is Anthropic's response to the growing concern of "AI burnout." When we interact with LLMs for hours on end, our brains mimic a high-speed feedback loop. We prompt, analyze, tweak, and prompt again. 

Located in the user profile menu, Reflect aggregates your usage statistics into easy-to-read metrics:
*   **Prompt Velocity:** How quickly you are sending follow-up prompts (high velocity often indicates frustration or aimless looping).
*   **Cognitive Load Index (CLI):** An estimation of your mental fatigue based on prompt complexity, session length, and time of day.
*   **The "Grass Index":** A playful, color-coded meter ranging from "Deep Focus" (Green) to "Diminishing Returns" (Yellow) to "Touch Grass" (Flashing Amber).

By analyzing these metrics, you can identify your peak productivity hours and prevent the creative stagnation that happens when you rely too heavily on AI generation.

## How to Configure and Use the Reflect Dashboard

Getting started with the Reflect dashboard is straightforward, but customizing it to fit your specific work habits is where the real magic happens.

### Step 1: Access the Dashboard
Click on your profile icon in the bottom-left corner of the Claude interface and select **Reflect Dashboard**. This will open a clean, minimalist analytics pane.

### Step 2: Set Your "Focus Windows"
By default, Claude assumes a standard 9-to-5 workday. If you are a night owl or a freelance developer, adjust your core hours in the settings. Claude will weigh evening sessions more heavily on the Cognitive Load Index, recognizing that late-night AI benders quickly lead to sleep disruption.

### Step 3: Enable "Touch Grass" Notifications
Under the notification tab, toggle on *Smart Interventions*. When your Grass Index hits the amber zone, Claude will gently suggest pausing the chat. If you ignore this and continue prompting, the AI will begin delivering shorter, more concise summaries, encouraging you to wrap up the session.

### Step 4: Analyze Your Prompt Loops
Look at the "Looping Alerts" section. If the dashboard shows you spent 45 minutes refactoring the same block of code with 12 consecutive prompts, it means you've hit a wall. This is your cue to walk away and let your subconscious do the problem-solving.
