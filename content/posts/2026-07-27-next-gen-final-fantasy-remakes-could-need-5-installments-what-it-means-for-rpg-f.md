## Introduction: The Unprecedented Scale of Modern JRPG Remakes

When Square Enix originally announced that the *Final Fantasy VII* remake project would be split into three standalone games, the gaming world reacted with a mix of excitement and skepticism. Today, with *Final Fantasy VII Remake* and *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* critically acclaimed, that multi-part format has proven to be a massive narrative and commercial success. However, recent discussions from key Square Enix developers indicate that three parts might actually be the lower limit for future projects.

In recent developer commentary regarding potential remakes of older legacy titles—most notably *Final Fantasy VI*—producers admitted that doing full, current-generation 3D reconstructions of classic 16-bit or 32-bit titles could take "4 or maybe even 5 installments" to properly realize. As we step further into 2025, RPG fans are faced with a fascinating reality: if you want your favorite nostalgic Final Fantasy title brought to life with modern graphics, prepare for an epic, decade-long commitment.

## Why Classic Games Require 4 or 5 Parts Today

To understand why a game like *Final Fantasy VI* or *Final Fantasy IX* would require four to five separate releases, one must look at how game development has evolved. The original 16-bit sprite games were immense in scope, featuring world maps, dozens of cities, huge character rosters, and global apocalyptic events. Back in the 1990s, these could be represented using abstract 2D tiles and simple character animations.

Translating those abstract elements into modern photorealistic high-definition environments requires an astronomical amount of asset creation. *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* pushed the PlayStation 5 to its absolute limits just to render a portion of its world map. A game like *Final Fantasy VI*, which features 14 permanent playable characters and a mid-game world-altering event that changes every location on the globe (the World of Ruin), would easily dwarf *FF7* in terms of raw environment and character density.

Dividing such a behemoth into 4 or 5 full-length AAA installments would be the only way for developers to avoid cutting massive amounts of content. Each entry would essentially function as its own open-world or semi-open-world RPG with 50 to 100 hours of gameplay.

## Prime Candidates: FFVI, FFIX, and Beyond

Which games are actually in line for this treatment?

1. **Final Fantasy VI**: Widely considered a masterpiece of the 16-bit era, *FFVI* boasts one of the largest ensembles in JRPG history. Rebuilding the steampunk city of Vector, the opera house, and the dual World of Balance / World of Ruin would easily demand a 4-to-5-part structure to preserve the narrative integrity.
2. **Final Fantasy IX**: Long rumored to have a remake in development, *FFIX* features distinct theatrical aesthetics, detailed continents, and complex worldbuilding. While it might fit into a trilogy, a complete overhaul featuring fully expanded kingdoms could stretch across four entries.
3. **Final Fantasy VIII**: With its complex gunblade combat mechanics, massive military academies, and time-bending storyline, *FFVIII* presents immense visual and design challenges that could easily justify a multi-part saga.

## The Cost of Ambition: Developer Fatigue and Fan Patience

While the concept of four or five ultra-detailed games based on a beloved classic sounds incredible on paper, it raises valid concerns regarding production timelines and gamer fatigue. If each installment takes 3 to 4 years to develop, a 5-part remake project could span nearly 20 years from start to finish.

Gamers who played the initial release in their 20s might be approaching their 40s by the time the finale drops. Additionally, keeping sales momentum high across five sequential games is a huge financial gamble for publishers. However, if the narrative depth and gameplay innovations match the standard set by the *FF7* trilogy, dedicated fans will almost certainly follow the journey through to the end.

## Our Verdict / Bottom Line

The idea of a 4 or 5-installment remake project proves that Square Enix views its classic library as legendary mythology worth preserving at the highest artistic level. While a multi-part release schedule requires immense patience and serious financial investment from fans, the result is an unprecedented level of immersion and detail that single-game remakes simply cannot match. If you are ready for the future of epic JRPGs, updating your display and setup in 2025 is the best way to prepare for the massive journeys ahead.

## Чому класичні ігри сьогодні потребують 4 або 5 частин

Щоб зрозуміти, чому така гра, як *Final Fantasy VI* або *Final Fantasy IX*, вимагатиме від чотирьох до п'яти окремих релізів, варто подивитися на еволюцію розробки ігор. Оригінальні 16-бітні спрайтові ігри мали колосальний масштаб: карти світу, десятки міст, величезні списки персонажів та глобальні апокаліптичні події. У 1990-х роках усе це можна було зобразити за допомогою абстрактних 2D-тайлів і простої анімації.

Перенесення цих абстрактних елементів у сучасні фотореалістичні середовища високої чіткості вимагає астрономічної кількості графічних асетів. *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* змусила PlayStation 5 працювати на межі можливостей лише для того, щоб відтворити частину карти світу. А така гра, як *Final Fantasy VI*, що має 14 постійних ігрових персонажів і глобальну подію посередині сюжету, яка повністю змінює кожну локацію на планеті (Світ Руїни / World of Ruin), легко затьмарить *FF7* за щільністю локацій та персонажів.

Поділ такого гіганта на 4 або 5 повноцінних AAA-частин був би єдиним способом для розробників уникнути вирізання величезного обсягу контенту. Кожна частина фактично функціонувала б як окрема RPG у відкритому або напіввідкритому світі з 50–100 годинами геймплею.
