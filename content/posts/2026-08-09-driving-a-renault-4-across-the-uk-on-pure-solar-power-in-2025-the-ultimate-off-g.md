## The Wild Challenge: LEJOG on Pure Sunlight

Crossing the entire length of the United Kingdom—from the windswept cliffs of Land's End in Cornwall to the rugged tip of John o' Groats in northern Scotland—is a legendary rite of passage. Covering roughly 874 miles across unpredictable weather, steep gradient shifts, and endless stretches of highway, doing the route in a modern electric vehicle (EV) is usually a simple matter of navigating from one fast charger to the next.

In 2025, however, we wanted to attempt something far more radical: completing the entire LEJOG journey in the newly released, retro-futuristic **Renault 4 E-Tech**, powered *exclusively* by solar energy. No national grid plugs, no commercial fast-charging networks, and no petrol generators. Just pure, unadulterated photons captured directly from the sky.

Here is the story of how an iconic French classic reborn for the modern era tackled Britain's unpredictable weather, and what this experiment tells us about the future of self-sustaining mobility.

## The Vehicle: Reimagining the Renault 4 E-Tech

The classic Renault 4 was built for rugged simplicity, utilitarian charm, and rough countryside roads. The 2025 Renault 4 E-Tech mirrors that spirit with modern electrification. Featuring a 52 kWh battery pack, an efficient 110 kW (150 hp) front-mounted motor, and a retro crossover stance, it delivers an official WLTP range of approximately 250 miles under normal conditions.

To prep the car for our solar-only expedition, we fitted a ultra-lightweight, custom flexible solar film array onto the roof and bonnet. However, because roof panels on a compact crossover can only generate about 200W to 300W under ideal sunlight, we knew that rooftop solar alone wouldn't cut it for timed travel. To actually cover real mileage, we carried high-efficiency deployable ground solar arrays and intermediate battery storage banks in the spacious boot.

## The Strategy: Patience, Efficiency, and Solar Tracking

Driving on solar power requires a total mindset shift. Instead of driving at 70 mph and stopping for 20 minutes at a 150 kW DC fast charger, solar road-tripping is a game of strategic energy management.

Our routine was simple yet demanding:
1. **Morning Harvest:** Unfold portable high-wattage solar arrays at sunrise while making coffee.
2. **Targeted Driving:** Drive at an energy-efficient 45–50 mph during late morning, utilizing regen braking through the rolling hills of Somerset and the Lake District.
3. **Midday Solar Peak:** Park in unshaded locations between 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM, deploying maximum panel wattage to feed intermediate power stations that stepped up voltage to slow-charge the Renault 4 via Level 1 AC connection.
4. **Evening Stride:** Drive the final leg before sunset and camp overnight.

The British weather lived up to its reputation. We experienced blistering sun in the Cotswolds, heavy overcast cloud cover through Lancashire, and intermittent drizzle across the Scottish Highlands. On sunny days, we harvested up to 8.5 kWh of energy. On overcast days, output dropped to a modest 2.2 kWh, forcing us to stay parked and wait for the skies to clear.

## Recommended Gear for Solar EV Expeditions

If you are planning an off-grid EV road trip, overlanding expedition, or emergency solar setup, having reliable solar and power storage gear is paramount. Here are the exact tier-one products we relied on during our 874-mile journey:

### 1. Renault 4 E-Tech Electric (2025)
* **Approximate Price:** £28,000 / $35,000 (Base Model)
* **Why it matters:** Excellent packaging, highly efficient heat pump, and compact footprint make it ideal for low-speed efficiency and light utility off-roading.

### 2. Anker SOLIX F3800 Portable Power Station
* **Approximate Price:** £3,200 / $3,999
* **Why it matters:** With a massive 3.84 kWh capacity and 120V/240V dual-voltage output, the SOLIX F3800 served as our primary intermediate energy reservoir. It accepted high PV input from our solar panels and safely discharged clean power into the Renault 4's onboard charger.

### 3. Bluetti PV350 350W Foldable Solar Panel (x4 units)
* **Approximate Price:** £650 / $799 each
* **Why it matters:** These portable panels feature impressive 23.4% solar cell efficiency and splash-proof ETFE coating. Chained together, four of these provided 1,400W of theoretical peak output during midday stops.

### 4. EcoFlow 220W Bifacial Portable Solar Panel
* **Approximate Price:** £360 / $449
* **Why it matters:** Bifacial design captures ambient light reflected from the ground on the rear side, boosting total harvest by up to 25% on concrete or light gravel surfaces.

### 5. Lectron Level 1 Portable EV Charger (16 Amp / 110V-240V)
* **Approximate Price:** £160 / $199
* **Why it matters:** A robust, adjustable-draw portable EVSE is essential when pulling power from portable power stations to prevent overloading the inverter.

## Lessons Learned: What 874 Miles on Solar Teaches Us

After 14 days on the road, we finally rolled up to the iconic signpost at John o' Groats. Our total grid electricity consumed: **0.0 kWh**. 

Here are three key takeaways from the trip:

* **Low Drag and Light Weight Win:** The Renault 4's relatively low kerb weight compared to massive electric SUVs was crucial. Conserving energy meant every single watt captured translate directly into extra range.
* **Solar Tech is Ready for Off-Grid Backup, Not Speed:** While waiting 2 to 3 days to fully replenish an EV battery purely on solar isn't practical for daily commuters, it proves that complete grid independence is achievable in remote environments.
* **Vehicle-to-Load (V2L) Integration is Key:** The 2025 Renault 4's built-in bidirectional charging capability allowed us to run camp gear directly off the car's main battery when needed, creating a closed-loop energy ecosystem.

## Bottom Line / Our Verdict

Driving a 2025 Renault 4 E-Tech the entire length of the UK powered solely by the sun was equal parts crazy, exhausting, and deeply rewarding. It exposed the raw reality of renewable energy availability while highlighting just how efficient modern EVs have become.

While solar-powered EV travel won't replace standard highway rapid-charging for modern holiday travel anytime soon, it proves that off-grid exploration is no longer a fantasy. The combination of lightweight compact EVs like the Renault 4 and high-output portable solar technology opens up unprecedented freedom for adventure seekers worldwide.

**Final Score:** 4.5 / 5 Stars for the Renault 4 E-Tech as a versatile, eco-conscious retro road-tripper.
