## Introduction

It is 2025, and Cyberpunk 2077 is widely regarded as one of the finest action role-playing games ever made. Thanks to the massive 2.0 overhaul, the introduction of path tracing, and the critically acclaimed *Phantom Liberty* expansion, Night City has finally become the immersive sci-fi sandbox we were promised nearly half a decade ago. 

Yet, the ghost of December 2020 still haunts the halls of CD Projekt Red (CDPR). 

In a recent, surprisingly candid interview, CD Projekt Red’s joint CEO, Michał Nowakowski, reflected on the long-term damage caused by the game's disastrous initial release. Despite spending tens of millions of dollars to fix the game and salvage their reputation, Nowakowski admitted a harsh truth: some players are gone forever. 

"I'm convinced that we lost the faith of some people indefinitely," Nowakowski stated. "There are people who will never buy another game of ours, and I have to live with that."

This level of executive transparency is rare in the gaming industry. Let’s dive into why this admission matters, how CDPR is changing its strategy for the upcoming *Witcher* sequel (Project Polaris), and the best gaming gear you need in 2025 to experience Cyberpunk 2077 the way it was always meant to be played.

---

## The Scar That Won't Heal

To understand Nowakowski’s comments, we have to look back at the sheer scale of the *Cyberpunk 2077* launch disaster. It wasn't just a buggy release; it was a cultural event of disappointment. 

Pre-release marketing promised a revolutionary next-generation RPG. Instead, players on PlayStation 4 and Xbox One received a borderline unplayable slideshow. The game crashed constantly, key features were missing, and the performance was so poor that Sony took the unprecedented step of delisting the game from the PlayStation Store for months.

While PC players with high-end rigs had a better experience, the console market felt betrayed. CDPR went from being the golden child of the gaming industry—celebrated for the pro-consumer masterpiece *The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt*—to a symbol of corporate greed and over-hyped marketing.

Nowakowski acknowledges that while millions of players returned for the 2.0 update and *Phantom Liberty*, a certain segment of the gaming community will never forgive the deception. For these gamers, no amount of free patches or redemptive DLC can erase the memory of a broken $60 pre-order.

---

## Can CD Projekt Red Rebuild Trust with Project Polaris?

Nowakowski’s admission isn't just a retrospective apology; it is a guiding philosophy for CDPR’s future. The studio is currently hard at work on *Project Polaris*, the codename for the next major entry in *The Witcher* franchise.

To ensure they never repeat the mistakes of 2020, CDPR is making radical structural changes:

* **Ditching REDengine for Unreal Engine 5:** One of the main bottlenecks of Cyberpunk’s development was fighting their proprietary engine. By partnering with Epic Games to use Unreal Engine 5, CDPR can focus on game design and stability rather than engine maintenance.
* **No More Last-Gen Anchors:** *Project Polaris* is being built from the ground up exclusively for modern hardware, ensuring they won't have to compromise their vision to fit on outdated consoles.
* **Humbler Marketing:** Expect the marketing cycle for the next Witcher game to be much shorter and more gameplay-focused, avoiding the years of cinematic hype that set unrealistic expectations for Cyberpunk.

---

## Our Verdict: The Bottom Line

Michał Nowakowski is right. Trust is the most valuable currency in the gaming industry, and once it is spent, you cannot simply buy it back with a patch. For some gamers, CD Projekt Red will always carry a asterisk.

However, judging the studio solely by its past mistakes ignores one of the greatest redemption arcs in entertainment history. Cyberpunk 2077 in 2025 is a triumph of engineering and storytelling. If you have the hardware to run it—whether that's a high-end RTX 40-series PC or a PlayStation 5—you owe it to yourself to experience Night City. 

CDPR has paid its dues in sweat, tears, and millions of dollars. While we should always remain healthily skeptical of pre-orders, the lessons learned from Cyberpunk's launch might just make *The Witcher 4* one of the most polished launches of the decade.
