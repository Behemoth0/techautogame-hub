## Introduction: The Hot Hatch Formula Gets Big

For decades, the hot hatch formula was beautifully simple: take a sensible, compact hatchback, stuff a turbocharged engine under the bonnet, stiffen the suspension, and give drivers a car that could tackle the weekday commute and weekend backroads with equal enthusiasm. The Volkswagen Golf GTI became the undisputed gold standard of this philosophy.

Fast forward to 2025, and life looks a bit different for many enthusiast drivers. Kids, dogs, camping gear, and hardware store runs mean a three-door hatch just doesn't cut it anymore. But does moving to a large family SUV mean surrendering your passion for a spirited drive?

Enter the 2025 Skoda Kodiaq vRS. Sharing its core architecture with the wider Volkswagen Group family, this seven-seater crossover claims to deliver genuine performance DNA in a package big enough for the whole family. I took the keys to a brand-new Kodiaq vRS for an early-morning Sunday drive across some of the finest twisty B-roads to find out if it truly earns the title of a "big Golf GTI."

## Under the Bonnet: Familiar EA888 Power

The comparison to the iconic GTI isn't just marketing fluff—it's mechanical reality. At the heart of the 2025 Skoda Kodiaq vRS is the latest iteration of the legendary EA888 2.0-litre turbocharged four-cylinder petrol engine. This is the exact same engine family that powers the modern Golf GTI and Golf R.

In the Kodiaq vRS, this powerplant generates an impressive 261 horsepower (195 kW) and 400 Nm of torque. Power is routed through a fast-shifting 7-speed DSG dual-clutch automatic gearbox and an intelligent all-wheel-drive (AWD) system.

While the extra bulk of a seven-seat SUV means it won't quite match a hatch in a straight-line sprint, the 0-62 mph (0-100 km/h) time of around 6.3 seconds is undeniably brisk for a vehicle of this size. Pitch it into a gap in traffic or overtake slow-moving caravans on a single carriageway, and the linear surge of mid-range torque feels instantly familiar to anyone who has driven a modern VW hot hatch.

## The Sunday Drive: Winding Roads and Surprising Agility

Setting off at 6:00 AM on a misty Sunday morning provided the perfect playground. The tarmac was damp, the turns were sharp, and there wasn't another car in sight.

Slipping the Kodiaq vRS into Sport mode immediately tightens the steering, sharpens throttle response, and firms up the standard Dynamic Chassis Control (DCC) adaptive dampers. As I tipped the big Skoda into a fast double-apex right-hander, the initial sensation was surprising: turn-in is sharp, direct, and precise.

Thanks to the electro-mechanical progressive steering, you get a decent sense of front-end grip. Body roll is kept impressively in check for a vehicle standing over 1.6 metres tall. Where a standard family crossover would float and understeer off line, the Kodiaq vRS grips, sets its outer tires, and powers out of the corner with zero fuss.

The all-wheel-drive system plays a crucial role here. Unlike the front-wheel-drive Golf GTI, which can sometimes struggle for traction on wet tarmac, the vRS shunts torque to the rear wheels the moment it detects slip. The result is point-and-shoot confidence regardless of weather conditions.

## Practicality Meets Performance: Living with the Beast

Where the Kodiaq vRS undeniably beats any hatchback is inside the cabin. Sinking into the high-backed bucket seats—trimmed in perforated microsuede with contrast red stitching—you get an immediate sense of sportiness without sacrificing long-distance comfort.

The 2025 model updates bring a massive 13-inch central infotainment display, customizable Virtual Cockpit digital dials, and Skoda's clever customizable 'Smart Dials' for quick climate control adjustments while on the move.

Crucially, you still get three rows of seating. While the rearmost seats are best reserved for children or short trips for adults, having seven seats available turns this performance machine into an ultimate daily driver. Fold the rear rows flat, and you are rewarded with a mammoth 2,065 litres of cargo capacity—enough to haul bicycles, furniture, or a full set of track day gear.

## How It Compares: Key Alternatives and Pricing

If you are considering a practical performance vehicle in 2025, the market offers several compelling options across different price points:

1. **Skoda Kodiaq vRS (2025)**
   * **Price:** ~$58,000 / £46,500
   * **The Verdict:** The benchmark for drivers needing 7-seat capability combined with genuine hot-hatch engine performance.

2. **Volkswagen Golf GTI (2025)**
   * **Price:** ~$32,995 / £39,800
   * **The Verdict:** The classic choice. Lighter, lower, and slightly more nimble, but limited by smaller cabin dimensions and 5-seat capacity.

3. **Cupra Formentor VZx (2025)**
   * **Price:** ~$52,000 / £45,000
   * **The Verdict:** A sharper, more aggressively styled crossover with 306 hp, though it only seats five and lacks the Kodiaq's cavernous interior space.

4. **Volkswagen Tiguan R-Line 2.0 TSI (2025)**
   * **Price:** ~$44,000 / £42,000
   * **The Verdict:** A polished, premium choice within the VW family, but lacks the raw punch and dedicated suspension tuning of the vRS badge.

## The Bottom Line: Our Verdict

So, is the Skoda Kodiaq vRS truly a "big Golf GTI"? 

Dynamically, physics cannot be completely rewritten. A 1.8-tonne, seven-seat SUV will never flick through tight chicanes with the lightweight agility of a 1.4-tonne hatchback. However, Skoda's engineers have managed to capture 85% of the Golf GTI's soul, steering precision, and punchy character, wrapping it in a vehicle that is ten times more practical.

For driving enthusiasts who have hit a stage in life where space and family duties take priority, the 2025 Skoda Kodiaq vRS is an outstanding compromise. It proves that you don't have to give up your favorite Sunday morning backroad drives just because you need room for seven. It is fast, capable, incredibly practical, and surprisingly fun when pushed hard.

**Final Score:** 4.5 / 5 - *The ultimate family-sized hot hatch substitute.*

## Під капотом: Знайома потужність EA888

Порівняння з культовим GTI — це не просто маркетинговий хід, а механічна реальність. У серці Skoda Kodiaq vRS 2025 року б'ється остання ітерація легендарного 2,0-літрового чотирициліндрового бензинового турбодвигуна EA888. Це те саме сімейство силових агрегатів, які встановлюються на сучасні Golf GTI та Golf R.

У Kodiaq vRS цей двигун видає вражаючі 261 к. с. (195 кВт) та 400 Нм крутного моменту. Потужність передається через швидку 7-ступінчасту автоматичну коробку передач DSG із подвійним зчепленням та інтелектуальну систему повного приводу (AWD).

Хоча зайва маса семимісного SUV заважає йому зрівнятися з хетчбеком у спринті по прямій, розгін 0–100 км/год приблизно за 6,3 секунди є беззаперечно швидким для авто таких габаритів. Вклинитися в потік або обігнати повільну колону на двосмуговій дорозі — і лінійний приплив крутного моменту на середніх обертах здасться миттєво знайомим кожному, хто кермував сучасним хот-хетчем VW.
