## The Long Road Back to the Old Republic

For Star Wars fans, few games hold as legendary a status as *Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic* (KOTOR). Originally released by BioWare in 2003, its masterclass storytelling, unforgettable twist, and deep RPG mechanics set a benchmark for the entire industry. So, when Sony and Aspyr Media announced a ground-up remake during the 2021 PlayStation Showcase, the gaming community erupted in collective joy.

Fast forward to today, and that joy has transitioned into a test of ultimate patience. Following a rocky development cycle, a shift in developers from Aspyr to Saber Interactive, and Saber's subsequent departure from Embracer Group, updates on the project have been agonizingly scarce. 

Now, we finally have a status update, but it might not be the news fans were hoping for. Saber Interactive's newly appointed business chief recently hinted in an industry interview that the KOTOR remake will "hopefully" be ready by **2028**. 

Yes, you read that right: 2028. That is nearly a quarter-century after the original game's release and seven years after the remake's initial announcement. 

## Why is the KOTOR Remake Taking Until 2028?

To understand this massive timeline, we have to look at the turbulent history of the project. The game was initially being handled by Aspyr, a studio best known for ports rather than massive, AAA ground-up remakes. After vertical slices of the game reportedly failed to impress Sony and Disney executives, the project was put on indefinite hold before being handed over to Saber Interactive.

When Saber Interactive parted ways with Embracer Group in a massive $247 million deal, they took the KOTOR remake with them. According to Saber's leadership, the game is indeed still "alive and well," but rebuilding a game of this scale requires starting almost entirely from scratch. 

Modernizing a game as vast as KOTOR—converting its turn-based, d20-style combat into something modern audiences will love, while preserving the cinematic narrative—is a monumental task. If 2028 is the realistic target, Saber is giving themselves the runway needed to avoid another *Cyberpunk 2077* or *Star Wars Jedi: Survivor* launch-day performance disaster.
