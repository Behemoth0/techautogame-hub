## Introduction

The UK new car market has reached a major milestone, recording its strongest July performance since before the COVID-19 pandemic. Driven by an unrelenting wave of adoption in electrified powertrains, registration figures published by the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders (SMMT) show a decisive shift toward cleaner, smarter mobility. As 2025 progresses, UK consumers and commercial fleet operators alike are snapping up Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) and Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEVs) in record numbers, proving that the future of British motoring is undeniably electrified.

After years of supply chain disruptions, semiconductor shortages, and macroeconomic headwinds, this growth signal provides welcome news for manufacturers and dealerships. The July registration boost reflects both improved vehicle availability and a maturing market where electric options are becoming mainstream choices rather than niche alternatives.

## The Electric & Hybrid Surge: What Is Driving Growth?

This market resurgence isn't just a random bump; it is heavily anchored by fleet registrations, corporate tax incentives, and a growing willingness among private buyers to make the switch. In July, electrified vehicles captured an unprecedented slice of the total market share. Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs) and PHEVs saw double-digit growth year-on-year, while fully electric cars continued their steady upward trajectory.

Fleet operators, incentivized by low Benefit-in-Kind (BiK) tax rates, remain the primary powerhouse behind this surge. Businesses looking to meet corporate sustainability goals are rapidly transitioning their company car fleets to zero-emission models. Meanwhile, private buyers are gradually returning to showrooms, lured by broader model ranges, competitive financing offers, and an ever-improving public charging infrastructure across the UK.

## Top EV & Hybrid Models Shaping the UK Market in 2025

To understand what is driving these record figures, we need to look at the vehicles pulling in British buyers. From budget-friendly compacts to premium executive cruisers, here are five standout models leading the pack:

### 1. MG4 EV (Approx. £26,995)
The MG4 EV continues to be the ultimate budget champion in the UK electric market. Offering sharp styling, impressive driving dynamics, and a real-world range of up to 281 miles on extended-range models, it brings practical EV ownership down to a realistic price point. It has proven especially popular with private buyers switching to pure electric for the first time.

### 2. Toyota Yaris Cross Hybrid (Approx. £25,500)
For buyers who aren't ready for a full plug-in commitment, Toyota's self-charging hybrid technology remains immensely popular. The Yaris Cross Hybrid combines small-SUV practicality with exceptional real-world fuel economy (frequently exceeding 60 mpg). It requires zero charging infrastructure reliance, making it a stress-free option for urban commuters.

### 3. Tesla Model Y (Approx. £44,990)
Despite fierce competition, the Tesla Model Y maintains its stronghold as one of the UK's top-selling crossovers. Its class-leading software interface, spacious cabin, excellent efficiency, and seamless access to the Tesla Supercharger network make it a benchmark choice for families and business drivers.

### 4. Kia EV6 (Approx. £45,245)
With its striking crossover-coupe design and ultra-fast 800V charging architecture (capable of charging from 10% to 80% in just 18 minutes), the Kia EV6 remains a technology flagship in the mid-size EV segment. It offers up to 328 miles of range and a refined driving experience that rivals long-established premium brands.

### 5. BMW i4 (Approx. £50,750)
On the executive front, the BMW i4 has become a favorite among company car drivers. Combining traditional BMW rear-wheel-drive handling dynamics with a quiet, powerful electric drivetrain, the i4 offers a seamless bridge between classic luxury driving and modern electrification, boasting a range of up to 365 miles.

## Navigating the ZEV Mandate and Infrastructure Challenges

While the strong July figures are cause for celebration, the UK automotive sector still faces significant hurdles. Under the UK government's Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV) mandate, vehicle manufacturers are required to ensure that a rising percentage of their total sales are zero-emission vehicles each year.

Meeting these stringent targets requires aggressive discounting, subsidised finance packages, and targeted dealer promotions, particularly in the private retail sector where high upfront purchase costs and elevated interest rates have occasionally slowed private adoption relative to corporate fleets.

Furthermore, while public charging network expansion is accelerating—with thousands of new ultra-rapid chargers deployed across motorway service areas and key transit hubs—regional disparities persist. Ensuring equal access to reliable, convenient charging for urban residents without off-street parking remains a top policy priority for local councils and charging providers.

## What This Means for UK Car Buyers

If you are currently shopping for a new or used car in 2025, the competitive landscape works strongly in your favor:

- **Attractive Finance Deals:** Manufacturers under pressure to meet ZEV mandate thresholds are offering zero-percent APR deals and substantial deposit contributions on new electric models.
- **Expanding Used Market:** As thousands of early-generation company fleet EVs reach the end of three-year lease cycles, the second-hand EV market is booming, making pre-owned electric models far more affordable.
- **Lower Running Costs:** Despite fluctuating electricity prices, off-peak home charging tariffs remain significantly cheaper per mile than petrol or diesel equivalents.

## Our Verdict

The UK car market’s best July performance since before Covid is definitive proof that the shift toward electrified transport is irreversible. Driven by robust fleet demand, enticing hybrid choices, and an increasingly competitive lineup of long-range pure electric cars, 2025 is proving to be a watershed year for the UK motor industry. While public charging infrastructure must continue to grow to support this momentum, there has never been a better time for British motorists to explore the benefits of going electric.
