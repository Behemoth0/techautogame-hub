## Introduction: The Generational Evolution of an American Icon

When Henry Ford II unveiled the original Ford Mustang at the 1964 World's Fair in Flushing Meadows, New York, he created a pop-culture earthquake. The car was stylish, attainable, and unmistakably American. Yet, by modern automotive engineering benchmarks, that original pony car was structurally primitive: unassisted drum brakes, lap belts that were often optional, a rigid non-collapsible steering column, and virtually no structural crumple zones.

Fast-forward through generations of family stewardship to Edsel B. Ford II. A long-time board member, motorsports advocate, and genuine automotive custodian, Edsel II has championed a radically different standard for performance. In his worldview—and throughout the modern development cycle leading to the seventh-generation (S650) 2025 Mustang—speed is worthless without active occupant preservation. Where Henry’s car gave drivers untamed freedom with minimal protection, the modern Mustang guided by contemporary Ford leadership incorporates aerospace-grade safety cells, advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS), and lightning-fast predictive emergency interventions.

In this review, we break down how modern engineering rectifies the safety vulnerabilities of Henry's 1964 original, examine real-world crash safety ratings, and review the top active safety gear and equipment packages available to protect your ride today.

## From Death-Trap Dynamics to High-Strength Steel: A Structural Revolution

To appreciate how far the Mustang has come under modern engineering leadership, one must examine the anatomy of a crash in 1964 versus 2025.

In the mid-1960s, a frontal offset collision at 40 mph typically resulted in the engine block invading the footwell, while the solid steering shaft acted like a lance pointed directly at the driver's chest. Lateral impacts meant almost zero survival space inside the thin doors.

Today, the modern Ford Mustang chassis relies on ultra-high-strength boron steel in the A-pillars, B-pillars, and door beams. Front-end crash rails are designed with hydroformed progressive crumple stages that disperse kinetic energy outward around the passenger cell rather than through it. Inside, occupants are safeguarded by a dual-stage front airbag system, side-impact curtain bags, and driver knee airbags that deploy in milliseconds based on micro-sensor telemetry.

## ADAS in 2025: Ford Co-Pilot360 Takes the Wheel

While Henry Ford II’s original pony car forced drivers to rely exclusively on gut instincts and reflex, modern safety turns the car into an observant co-pilot. Ford's integrated active-safety suite—Ford Co-Pilot360—comes standard or as an upgraded package on modern Mustangs, directly mitigating the most common causes of high-performance car accidents.

Key active systems include:

- **Pre-Collision Assist with Automatic Emergency Braking (AEB):** Millimeter-wave radar and forward-facing optical cameras detect vehicles, pedestrians, and obstacles. If the driver does not respond to audible and visual alerts, the system primes the brake lines and applies full stopping pressure autonomously.
- **BLIS (Blind Spot Information System) with Cross-Traffic Alert:** Rear quarter visibility has historically been a weak point for sports coupes. Radar sensors mounted behind the rear bumper cover monitor the Mustang's blind zones and alert the driver via bright LED indicators in the side mirrors.
- **Lane-Keeping System with Road Edge Detection:** If you begin drifting across lane markers during spirited driving or highway fatigue, the electric power-assisted steering (EPAS) introduces corrective torque to steer you back toward center.
- **Post-Impact Braking:** An often-overlooked feature that automatically applies braking pressure following an initial collision, preventing catastrophic secondary impacts with other vehicles or guardrails.

## Crash Testing and IIHS/NHTSA Benchmarks

Modern safety cannot merely exist on an equipment sheet; it must prove itself on crash test sleds. While the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) awarded the sixth-generation platform 5-star overall ratings across frontal, side, and rollover metrics, modern Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) criteria have become drastically stricter.

Under updated side-impact testing featuring heavier, taller impact barriers (replicating modern full-size SUVs), structural integrity and side-curtain airbag coverage have become the defining differentiators. The modern Mustang platform addresses these demands with high-density side sill reinforcements—a stark, reassuring contrast to the paper-thin rocker panels that characterized original 1960s production.

## ADAS у 2025 році: Ford Co-Pilot360 бере контроль

Якщо оригінальний «поні-кар» Генрі Форда II змушував водіїв покладатися виключно на інтуїцію та реакцію, то сучасні системи безпеки перетворюють автомобіль на пильного другого пілота. Інтегрований комплекс активної безпеки Ford Co-Pilot360 входить до стандартної комплектації або доступний у вигляді пакета оновлень для сучасних Mustang, безпосередньо запобігаючи типовим причинам аварій на потужних авто.

Ключові активні системи включають:

- **Система попередження зіткнень з автоматичним екстреним гальмуванням (AEB):** міліметровий радар та оптичні камери переднього огляду виявляють автомобілі, пішоходів і перешкоди. Якщо водій не реагує на звукові та візуальні сигнали, система підвищує тиск у гальмівній магістралі та автономно задіює повне гальмування.
- **BLIS (моніторинг «сліпих» зон) із попередженням про перехресний рух позаду:** оглядовість задньої півсфери завжди була слабким місцем спортивних купе. Радарні датчики за заднім бампером контролюють «сліпі» зони Mustang і попереджають водія яскравими LED-індикаторами в бічних дзеркалах.
- **Система утримання в смузі руху з виявленням краю дороги:** якщо ви починаєте виїжджати за межі розмітки через азартне кермування або втому на шосе, електропідсилювач керма (EPAS) додає коригувальне зусилля, щоб повернути авто в центр смуги.
