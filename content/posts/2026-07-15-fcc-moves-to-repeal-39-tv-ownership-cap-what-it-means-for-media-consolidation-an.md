## Introduction

In a regulatory move that is set to reshape the American media landscape, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has initiated steps to repeal the long-standing 39% national television ownership cap. For decades, this cap prevented any single broadcast television station group from reaching more than 39% of U.S. television households. 

This deregulation is widely viewed as a major victory for large media conglomerates, particularly politically conservative and Trump-friendly news organizations like Sinclair Broadcast Group and Nexstar Media Group. By removing this barrier, these massive broadcasting networks can now acquire more local stations, expanding their national reach far beyond previous legal limits. 

As media consolidation accelerates in 2025, the way we consume local news, national broadcasts, and syndicated content is shifting. For cord-cutters and tech enthusiasts, this regulatory change highlights the importance of taking control of your own living room media setup. Whether you want to access free local over-the-air (OTA) broadcasts or curate your own diverse streaming news feeds, having the right hardware is essential.

---

## The FCC's Deregulation Shake-Up: What's Happening?

The 39% cap was originally designed to protect media pluralism, ensuring that local communities had access to diverse, independent viewpoints rather than a monoculture controlled by a few corporate giants. However, proponents of the repeal argue that the rule is an outdated relic of the pre-internet era. They contend that traditional broadcast stations are struggling to compete against unregulated big tech platforms, streaming giants like Netflix, and algorithm-driven social media feeds.

With the repeal of the cap, expect a massive wave of mergers and acquisitions in the television industry. A single parent company could soon own the local CBS, FOX, or NBC affiliates in nearly every major market across the country. For viewers, this means local news broadcasts will likely feature more centralized, nationally produced segments, shifting the editorial tone of local reporting.

For audiences who prefer to seek out diverse perspectives or maintain access to free, independent local programming, relying solely on standard cable packages is no longer the best strategy.
