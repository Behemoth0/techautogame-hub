## Introduction: Fixing the Bolt's Biggest Achilles' Heel

When General Motors introduced the original Chevrolet Bolt EV back in 2016 for the 2017 model year, it was a legitimate trailblazer. It beat Tesla to the punch with an affordable, 200-plus-mile mass-market electric vehicle. Drivers fell in love with its peppy acceleration, surprisingly roomy hatchback cabin, and rock-bottom lease deals. 

Yet, for all its early brilliance, the first-generation Bolt suffered from one unforgivable flaw: painfully slow DC fast charging. 

Capped at a theoretical maximum of 55 kW, road-tripping an older Bolt meant lingering at highway rest stops while newer rivals sped off in twenty minutes. Now, as GM readies the next-generation Chevy Bolt built on its modern Ultium (now GM EV) platform with cost-effective Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) cells and a native NACS charging port, charging speed is getting the overhaul it desperately needed. 

Here is a deep-dive comparison into how charging the next-gen Chevy Bolt stacks up against the beloved, but slow-sipping, original.

## The Baseline: How Slow Was the Old Chevy Bolt?

To appreciate the upgrade, we must first revisit the original Bolt EV and Bolt EUV charging architecture. 

Under ideal ambient temperatures, the first-generation Bolt could technically pull up to **55 kW** on a DC fast charger. In reality, that peak was fleeting. The battery management system tapered charging aggressively:

* **10% to 50%:** Reached peak charging near 50–54 kW.
* **50% to 70%:** Tapered down to roughly 38–40 kW.
* **70% to 80%:** Dropped further to roughly 24 kW.
* **80% and beyond:** Plummeted below 15 kW.

In real-world conditions, taking an older Bolt from 10% to 80% on a road trip required between **60 and 75 minutes**. If you were caught in a winter cold snap without active preconditioning, that time could easily stretch toward 90 minutes. Level 2 home charging was respectable—early models maxed out at 7.2 kW (about 10 hours for a full charge), while 2022 and newer models upgraded to 11.5 kW onboard chargers (roughly 7 hours on a 48-amp circuit).

## The New Chevy Bolt: What Ultium Architecture Brings

While GM retired the older BEV2 platform, the next-gen Bolt borrows proven hardware from siblings like the Equinox EV and Blazer EV. It shifts to advanced battery management, an LFP chemistry designed to withstand repeated 100% charges without rapid degradation, and native compatibility with Tesla’s Supercharger network via the NACS port.

### DC Fast Charging Speeds

Instead of the dated 55 kW ceiling, the next-gen Bolt adopts charging rates aligned with GM's mid-tier Ultium crossovers:

* **Peak Charge Rate:** Estimated at **150 kW** (nearly triple the original).
* **10% to 80% Charge Time:** Projected between **28 and 32 minutes** under optimal conditions.
* **Range Added in 10 Minutes:** Up to **70–80 miles**, compared to just ~35 miles in the original Bolt.

Cutting the 10-to-80% dwell time in half fundamentally transforms the Bolt from a strictly commuter vehicle into a legitimate weekend road-trip option. You can plug in, grab a quick lunch, use the restroom, and return to an 80% charge ready to roll.

### Level 2 Home Charging

For daily home charging, the new Bolt maintains GM’s standard **11.5 kW onboard charger**, requiring a 48-amp hardwired circuit for maximum throughput. It replenishes roughly 30 to 37 miles of range per hour of charge time, easily taking a completely drained battery back to 100% overnight in about 6.5 to 7.5 hours.

Whether you stick with your current Bolt or buy into the new model, having the right hardware makes EV ownership seamless. Here are top-tier accessories currently available on the market:

If you want to max out the 11.5 kW onboard charger on newer Bolts, the **Emporia 48-Amp Charger** is the benchmark for value. It hardwires directly into a 60-amp circuit, features robust Wi-Fi energy monitoring, and lets you schedule charges during off-peak utility hours to minimize electricity costs.

### 2. Grizzl-E Classic 40-Amp Level 2 EV Charger (~$349)
Built like a tank in an all-aluminum casing, the Canadian-made **Grizzl-E Classic** is legendary for outdoor installations in severe climates. It provides up to 9.6 kW (40 amps on a NEMA 14-50 plug), replenishing a depleted Bolt in around 8 hours without requiring hardwiring.

### 3. Lectron Vortex NACS to CCS1 Adapter (~$169)
If you are driving a first-gen Bolt and want access to thousands of Tesla Superchargers, the **Lectron Vortex Adapter** handles up to 500V and 500A. Even though your older Bolt will still charge at its 55 kW limit, it gives you peace of mind and access to the world's most reliable charging network.

### 4. Schumacher SC1455 Level 1/Level 2 Portable Charger (~$199)
A versatile emergency charger to throw in your trunk. It switches between a standard 120V household wall outlet and a 240V NEMA 6-20 socket, ensuring you can trickle-charge wherever an outlet exists.

## Our Verdict: The Bottom Line

The original Chevy Bolt will always hold a special place in electric vehicle history, but its 55 kW charging bottleneck made long trips a test of patience. 

By moving to modern EV architecture, boosting peak charging to approximately 150 kW, and integrating native NACS support, the next-gen Chevy Bolt eradicates the model's only serious flaw. Cutting highway stop times from over an hour down to roughly thirty minutes brings Chevrolet's budget-friendly EV in line with contemporary standards. If slow charging kept you from buying an older Bolt, the new generation is officially ready for your driveway.
