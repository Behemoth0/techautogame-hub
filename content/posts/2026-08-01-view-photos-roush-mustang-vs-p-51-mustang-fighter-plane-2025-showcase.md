## Introduction: Aviation Heritage Meets Modern Asphalt Superpowers

Few names in American history evoke the raw sense of speed, freedom, and mechanical prowess quite like the word "Mustang." Long before the Ford Mustang galloped onto the automotive scene in 1964, the North American P-51 Mustang dominated WWII skies as an unmatched long-range escort fighter. Decades later, legendary motorsport engineer Jack Roush bridged these two worlds, drawing direct inspiration from WWII aviation technology to build some of the most aggressive, high-performance Ford Mustangs ever to touch pavement.

In this exclusive photo tour and deep-dive comparison, we take an up-close look at the high-speed visual showdown between a supercharged Roush Mustang and its historic airborne namesake, the P-51 Mustang fighter plane. From riveted aluminum wings to carbon-fiber splitters, this comparison highlights how military aviation engineering continues to shape high-octane automotive performance in 2025.

## The Design Language: Polished Aluminum Meets Carbon Fiber

When you line up a classic WWII P-51D Mustang alongside a modern Roush Mustang on a sun-drenched tarmac, the visual connection is instant and breathtaking. The plane’s polished aluminum fuselage reflects the sunlight with blinding clarity, contrasted by yellow-tipped propeller blades and classic army insignia. 

Directly beside it, the Roush Mustang mirrors those exact fighter-jet aesthetics. Key photo highlights reveal:

- **Aggressive Aerodynamics:** The front fascia of the modern Roush Mustang features enlarged air intakes and a functional hood scoop engineered like a fighter aircraft jet inlet.
- **Aviation-Inspired Accents:** Modern Roush builds frequently pay tribute to the P-51 with metallic silver paint schemes, military olive accents, custom leather upholstery featuring fighter-pilot badging, and tactical green graphics.
- **Cockpit Ergonomics:** Inside the Roush, drivers are greeted by custom white-faced gauge pods, aircraft-style toggle switches, and a stubby cue-ball shifter that evokes the control stick of a vintage fighter craft.

## Under the Hood vs. Under the Engine Cowling

Comparing horsepower between an aircraft and a muscle car reveals two radically different expressions of mechanical violence.

### The P-51D Mustang Fighter Plane
Powered by the legendary 27-liter Packard V-1650-7 (a license-built Rolls-Royce Merlin V12), the P-51D churns out approximately 1,490 horsepower at takeoff, boosting up to nearly 1,720 horsepower with war emergency power. It cruises at speeds exceeding 430 mph at 25,000 feet, making it one of the fastest piston-driven aircraft in history.

### The Roush Mustang
The latest iteration of the Roush Mustang relies on Ford's 5.0-liter Coyote V8 paired with a massive TVS supercharger system. In Stage 3 trim, this road missile delivers over 800 horsepower and 670 lb-ft of torque directly to the rear wheels. While it won't fly, it flies from 0 to 60 mph in under 3.5 seconds with a supercharger whine that sounds uncannily like a Merlin V12 screaming down a runaway.

## Photo Highlights: Details You Can't Miss

In our high-resolution gallery review, several design details stand out:

1. **The Exhaust Port Alignment:** Notice how the side-mounted exhaust stacks on the P-51’s nose directly align in spirit with the quad-tip active exhaust system protruding from the Roush Mustang's rear diffuser.

### Винищувач P-51D Mustang
Оснащений легендарним 27-літровим двигуном Packard V-1650-7 (ліцензійна версія Rolls-Royce Merlin V12), P-51D видає приблизно 1490 кінських сил під час зльоту, збільшуючи потужність майже до 1720 к. с. у режимі бойової тривоги (war emergency power). Остання ітерація Roush Mustang покладається на 5,0-літровий двигун Coyote V8 від Ford у парі з масивною системою нагнітача TVS.
